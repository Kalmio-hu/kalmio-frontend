## One course is a meal

In the autumn plans every lunch and dinner became soup plus main. It looked handsome, it sounded Hungarian, and in one week it doubled the shopping and the cooking. For a person living alone, two courses twice a day is not a diet, it is a second job.

From now on a meal is one course. If you want soup before the main, switch it on under Settings › Courses for lunch, dinner or both. The planner asks that much and gives that much.

## Fourteen one-pot dishes that stand alone

If one course has to fill a 1 000 calorie lunch, that course cannot be a vegetable soup. So fourteen new recipes arrived, all complete meals with at least 38 grams of protein a serving: bean goulash with smoked pork neck, Jókai bean soup, lentil soup with sausage, chicken ragout soup, palóc soup, catfish paprikash, tarragon turkey ragout soup, cholent, székelygulyás, and five főzelék with a protein topping.

Cheap proteins: dried beans, lentils, yellow peas, eggs, chicken thigh, pork shoulder. No chicken breast in any of them. No ready-made stock in any of them: water and aromatics, at most one bouillon cube per litre.

## A daily budget

The planner has had a budget cap for a long time; no screen let you set it. Settings › Daily budget now exists, and we suggest 4 500 forints for two meals a day. The planner overshoots it by at most twenty percent, and the Budget weight decides how hard it stays under. The plan page now shows cost per day.

## Fewer packs

The planner now pays a small penalty for every distinct ingredient. Two recipes that share the same carrots make a better week than two that each bring something new. For one person shopping alone, that difference is ten packs and ten leftovers a week.
