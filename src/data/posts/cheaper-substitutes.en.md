## Savoy cabbage instead of kale

There is a kind of saving every Hungarian kitchen knows and no meal planner ever dared do for you: you do not swap the recipe, you swap the ingredient. Savoy cabbage instead of kale. A bouillon cube and water instead of ready-made stock. Chicken thigh instead of breast. A regular tomato instead of cherry tomatoes.

Kalmio now does this too, if you ask. Under Settings › Daily budget there is a switch: **Cost-saver substitutions**. It is off by default.

## What happens when you switch it on

Every line of the shopping list is checked: is there a cheaper substitute at the shops you switched on? If there is, the list buys the substitute and says what it replaces: "savoy cabbage – instead of kale". If the substitute is not cheaper, the original stays. The recipe does not change; a line under the recipe shows the swap and what to mind: stir sour cream in at the end, do not boil it.

The planner prices recipes with the same eyes. A dish that substitutes well counts as a cheaper dish and moves up under the daily budget.

## Where the pairs come from

From a table the chef maintains. Fourteen rules live today: kale, stock, chicken breast, turkey breast, beef, salmon, cherry tomatoes, basmati, brown rice, quinoa, cream, walnuts, sweet potato, bell pepper. Each has a ratio and one sentence on what the swap does to the dish. Saving a forint is not worth a bad dinner; if we would not put it on our own table, it is not in the list.

## What does not happen

We do not swap for allergies and we do not swap silently. Dietary substitutions go another route, and your list spells out every swap. The switch comes back off in one move.
