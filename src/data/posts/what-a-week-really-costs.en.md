## Three numbers, one week

A weekly plan in Kalmio can show three different amounts, and for a while we called all three "cost". That was the mistake.

- **What the recipes consume.** Every ingredient exactly as the recipe asks: 262 g of kale, 40 g of feta. The smallest number, and the one on the plan page.
- **What you take off the shelf.** Kale comes in a 300 g bag, feta in 200 g. The difference is leftover, and you pay for it at the till.
- **What the pantry staples take.** Spices, oil, vinegar, salt: whole jars that last for months. Heavy in week one, barely there afterwards.

A real week of ours looked like this: the recipes consumed 24 000 forints, leftovers added 17 000, pantry staples another ten. The till said 51 000. The plan page said 24.

## What changed

The shopping list's headline now says what you pay: whole packs, pantry staples included. Beneath it two smaller lines: how much the recipes use, and how much is left over because of pack sizes. A big leftover is the plan's fault, not yours, and the planner now learns that too: it is penalised when a week spans forty-four different ingredients, because forty-four packs leave forty-four tails.

## Three things the numbers taught us

Ready-made stock is the dearest line of a Hungarian week. Four and a half litres cost nine thousand forints, while bouillon cubes do the same job for a few hundred. Our recipes now use water and aromatics, not stock from the litre carton.

Premium products slip onto the list quietly. The system picked the "best matching" product, and the best match was often organic. Now we know every matching product and buy the cheapest pack.

A two-course lunch doubles the week. Soup and main every day means twice the ingredients and twice the packs. That is what the next post is about.
